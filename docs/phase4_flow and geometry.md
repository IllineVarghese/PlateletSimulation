# Phase 4 – Flow & Geometry

## Overview

Phase 4 focused on introducing realistic vascular flow conditions and vessel geometries into the Virtual Platelet Simulation framework. While previous phases concentrated on agent architecture, gene regulatory networks (GRNs), and behavior generation, this phase established the environmental context in which platelet agents operate. The primary objective was to simulate platelet transport under physiologically relevant blood-flow conditions and investigate how mechanical signals influence platelet behavior through the GRN-based control system.

---

## Objectives

The objectives of Phase 4 were:

1. Implement a parabolic flow profile (Poiseuille flow)
2. Compute local shear stress from the flow field
3. Validate flow behavior using a cone geometry
4. Import and analyze vessel meshes derived from microscopy pipelines
5. Simulate platelet behavior within vessel environments under realistic flow conditions

---

## Poiseuille Flow Implementation

A laminar flow model based on the Poiseuille velocity profile was implemented. The velocity is highest at the vessel centerline and gradually decreases toward the vessel walls, reproducing the characteristic behavior of blood flow in cylindrical vessels. This provided a realistic transport mechanism for platelet agents and served as the foundation for subsequent flow analyses.

### Achievements

- Implemented parabolic velocity distribution
- Simulated realistic platelet transport
- Validated velocity gradients across vessel radius
- Generated flow visualization outputs

---

## Shear Stress Computation

Local shear stress values were calculated from the velocity field and assigned to individual platelet agents. Shear stress acts as an important mechanosensory signal in platelet biology and was therefore integrated into the existing GRN framework as an environmental input.

### Achievements

- Computed local shear stress from flow field
- Connected shear sensing to platelet agents
- Integrated shear signals into GRN processing
- Generated shear-response analysis plots

---

## Cone Geometry Validation

A cone-shaped vessel geometry was introduced to evaluate simulation behavior in non-uniform vascular structures. The varying diameter generates spatial changes in velocity and shear stress, providing a useful validation scenario before transitioning to real vessel geometries.

### Achievements

- Implemented cone vessel geometry
- Tested flow behavior under changing diameter
- Observed expected velocity adaptations
- Verified stable agent transport

---

## Real Vessel Mesh Integration

Microscopy-derived vessel meshes were successfully imported and inspected. Mesh metadata, geometry dimensions, and structural information were extracted and analyzed. The imported meshes were integrated into the simulation workflow and used to generate vessel-derived simulation domains.

The implemented Phase 4 pipeline successfully demonstrated:

- Realistic platelet transport under Poiseuille flow

---

## Biological Relevance

Platelets continuously experience mechanical forces generated by blood flow. Shear stress is one of the most important physical signals influencing platelet activation, adhesion, aggregation, and thrombus formation.

By integrating flow mechanics with GRN-driven behavior, the simulation moves closer to representing biologically realistic platelet function and provides a foundation for future studies of:

- Hemostasis
- Thrombosis
- Platelet activation
- Mechanotransduction
- Flow-mediated signaling

---

## Limitations

The current implementation intentionally focuses on efficient simulation rather than full computational fluid dynamics.

Current limitations include:

- Prescribed flow fields rather than Navier–Stokes solutions
- Simplified vessel-wall interactions
- Mesh-derived vessel proxy representation
- No red blood cell simulation
- No plasma particle simulation
- No deformable platelet meshes

These limitations are appropriate for the current thesis scope and will serve as opportunities for future extensions.

---

## Conclusion

Phase 4 successfully established the environmental foundation required for realistic platelet simulations. Blood-flow dynamics, shear stress sensing, geometric constraints, and vessel mesh integration were incorporated into the simulation framework and coupled to the existing GRN-based behavior system.

The framework now supports biologically meaningful platelet behavior under realistic flow conditions and provides a robust platform for future visualization and biological extensions.
